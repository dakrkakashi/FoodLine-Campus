FoodLine Campus · Financial Model

How a ₹80 canteen order turns into ₹1.18 lakh a year in profit

A walk-through of the revenue, cost, and break-even math behind FoodLine Campus's pilot at a single 2,500-student campus.

2,500Total students
1,000Active users (40%)
5Orders / user / month
₹80Average order value

Order volume

Everything else in this model is downstream of how many orders the platform processes in a year.

Active users

Total students × Active-user % = 2,500 × 40% = 1,000 active users

Orders per month

Active users × Orders per user/month = 1,000 × 5 = 5,000 orders/month

Orders per year

Monthly orders × 12 = 5,000 × 12 = 60,000 orders/year

GMV — Gross Merchandise Value

GMV is the total value of food sold through the platform — not FoodLine's revenue. It's the base on which commission and payment-processing costs are calculated.

Annual orders × Average order value = 60,000 × ₹80 = ₹48,00,000 (₹48 Lakh/year)
Note: ₹48L is money changing hands between students and canteens. FoodLine only earns a slice of it.

Revenue — three streams

Every order earns roughly ₹7 in direct revenue, plus a flat monthly SaaS fee per participating canteen.

Commission (5% of GMV)

GMV × 5% = ₹48,00,000 × 5% = ₹2,40,000/year

Student convenience fee (₹3/order)

Orders × ₹3 = 60,000 × ₹3 = ₹1,80,000/year

SaaS fee (per canteen)

Modeled for one participating canteen account on this campus, at ₹2,000/month.

₹2,000 × 12 = ₹24,000/year

Revenue per order ≈ ₹7 — ₹4 commission + ₹3 convenience fee. SaaS revenue sits on top of this, independent of order volume.

COGS — cost of goods sold

The variable costs that scale directly with orders and GMV.

Payment gateway (1.8% of GMV)

GMV × 1.8% = ₹48,00,000 × 1.8% = ₹86,400/year

Order processing (₹1/order)

Covers SMS/notifications, operational handling, minor support, and order processing.

Annual orders × ₹1 = 60,000 × ₹1 = ₹60,000/year

Gross profit & margin

Revenue − COGS = ₹4,44,000 − ₹1,46,400 = ₹2,97,600
Gross Profit ÷ Revenue × 100 = ₹2,97,600 ÷ ₹4,44,000 × 100 = 67.03%
Discrepancy in the source notes: an earlier summary line states gross margin as ~69%, but the detailed calculation above (₹2,97,600 ÷ ₹4,44,000) works out to 67.03%. Worth reconciling before this goes into a pitch deck.

Break-even point

SaaS revenue (₹24,000) offsets part of OPEX before order volume needs to cover the rest.

Annual OPEX − SaaS revenue = ₹1,80,000 − ₹24,000 = ₹1,56,000

Dividing the remaining OPEX by contribution per order (₹4.56 — revenue per order minus variable cost per order):

₹1,56,000 ÷ ₹4.56 ≈ 34,211 orders/year
Break-even ≈ 34,200 orders/year, roughly equivalent to ₹27 Lakh in GMV/year — well under the modeled 60,000-order pilot volume.

Cash flow

Under simple assumptions — digital payments, controlled canteen settlement, no major working-capital drag, no large one-time investment — operating cash flow tracks net profit closely.

Operating cash flow ≈ ₹1,17,600/year (≈ Net Profit)

With an initial build cost

If ₹1 lakh is spent upfront building the system, the first year looks different:

₹1,17,600 − ₹1,00,000 = ₹17,600
Takeaway: the underlying business can be profitable while the first-year cash position is much thinner — the gap is entirely the one-time build investment, not the operating economics.
